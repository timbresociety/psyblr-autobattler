Kit library, and four sessions to close the game

Every button's library as it stands in the code today (307 items, generated from src/charter/templates), what it overlaps and what it misses for third-person hero combat, then the plan in the owner's order: lock the kit, build it and check it on today's map, rebuild the map, then the generative forge. 2026-11-04.

Is the library MECE? Not yet.

  • Not mutually exclusive. Transformations sit in both F and R under the same names; four E skills repeat the shared moves; some Q bursts repeat LMB weapons; the summon family holds no summons; stun lives outside the other controls.
  • Not collectively exhaustive for the genre. The hero-shooter core is well covered. Missing is most of what the anime and superhero fandoms come for: flight, swinging, teleporting to a mark, counters, clones, minions, copying a kit, grabs and throws, moveset-changing forms, revives and team-ups. Most need a new engine piece, not just new entries.

V

Melee weapon styles 7

Tap ×3 for the combo (the 3rd hit knocks back), hold for the charged heavy, X to guard. A style sets the clips, reach, arc and lunge; damage comes from the role. Every role may take every style.

Overlaps (not mutually exclusive)

  • Spear, whip and chain share Axe or staff with axes and staffs, though they move differently (thrust, crack and wrap against swing).

Missing for the genre (not exhaustive)

  • Dual blades and claws (Wolverine)
  • Spear or lance as its own thrusting style
  • Whip or chain as its own style
  • Scythe
  • Power gauntlets (could stay Unarmed with heavier blows)
  • An air juggle on the chain: launch, chase, slam (Sparking Zero, MHUR)
  • Grab, carry and throw (Lash's Death Slam, Sparking Zero throws)

Spells 107

Everything no other row covers, in ten families: Burst 13 · Control 23 · Displacement 13 · Curse 14 · Zone 7 · Construct 4 · Summon 12 · Support 11 · Vision 6 · Trick 4.

Overlaps (not mutually exclusive)

  • The summon family holds no summons: it is turrets, traps and totems, which overlap construct and zone.

  • A directional barrier that blocks shots (Doctor Strange, Magneto)
  • Absorbing projectiles (Magneto's ultimate)
  • Walkable terrain: ramps, ice paths, platforms (Todoroki)
  • Ability charges: two or three uses banked

Plan

Four sessions to close the game

The owner's order, 2026-11-04. Each session ends with something the owner signs or plays. Movement feel is not matched to MHUR; we may differ. Every session runs the same gates (tsc, the fast suites, the balance gate alone, the build, the Vercel-shaped build, and a look with our own eyes) and records its decisions in GAME.md.

Session 1Clip audit, and a locked MECE kit library
Session 2Build the kit, then the owner's check on today's map
Session 3Rebuild the map around the kit
Session 4The generative forge: bodies, weapons, projectiles

1 · Clip audit, and a locked kit library

Know that every clip we play reads right on every body, and turn the six button libraries into one list with no overlaps and no genre gaps the owner cares about.

Do

  1. An audit harness that scores each clip without a person watching: root drift, hip sway, foot slide, bone pops, loop seams and the travel line. It renders a contact sheet per clip on tiny, medium and huge bodies, female and male.
  2. Audit every clip in use: the shared moves (the table above), seven melee styles (three hits, heavy, guard), ranged in each stance, the four E kinds, the F toggle, R, hit reacts, knock-downs. Each gets keep, trim, replace or missing.
  3. Fix the five overlaps: forms in one row, E without baseline repeats, Q burst set apart from LMB, summon given real summons, stun folded into control.
  4. Write the library as a grid per button (delivery × effect group) so every item has exactly one cell, and list each gap as a new engine piece with a size and a yes or no.
  5. Map it to bodies and archetypes: the clip each item plays per size class, the default kits per role, and which melee styles and ranged kinds each body may hold.

The owner decides

  • Which new pieces go in: teleport, counter, flight, clones and summons, moveset forms, grab and throw, air juggle, revive, charges, charge casts.
  • The form rule: F only, or F toggles and R is the bigger, timed form.
  • Whether E drops Wall Run, Combat Roll, Power Slide and Zipline.
  • New melee styles: spear, whip or chain, dual blades or claws, scythe.

Out of scope

  • Feel-matching to MHUR, map work, generated meshes.
Exit: the owner signs the locked library in GAME.md §4, with an audit report in which every clip in use passes or has its replacement named.

2 · Build the kit, then the owner's check on today's map

Everything signed in session 1 works in play, on every body, for players and bots, on the current map.

Do

  1. Build the approved pieces in build order (teleport and counters first), each with engine tests and a test that fails without it.
  2. Re-cut the catalogue to the locked grid: rename, move and remove entries; new entries for the new pieces.
  3. Swap in the replacement clips from the audit, and check every new move in the seat on tiny, medium and huge bodies with filmstrips.
  4. Teach the bots to use each new piece.
  5. Rerun the balance gate; retune only with the owner's say-so.

The owner decides

  • Combat and traversal on the current map: the owner's own check, and the fix list that comes from it.
  • Any retune the balance gate asks for.

If it runs over

  • Pieces later in the build order move to a short opening of session 3; the map does not start on an unchecked kit.
Exit: the owner has played combat and traversal on today's map and the fix list is closed.

3 · Rebuild the map around the kit

A map built for the moves we now have, in the arcade look, that every body size can travel.

Do

  1. A look-test corner first: the arcade direction (casino banners and indoor plants are fine, no Roman arena), lit and checked in renders.
  2. A grey-box layout made for the kit: ledges above 4.5 m where a grab should beat a mantle, marked walls for wall-runs, zipline and rail lines, room to fly if flight went in, sight lines for marksmen, low cover for crouch and prone.
  3. A traversal pass on every size class: each route checked in the seat.
  4. Model and bake in Blender, then navigation for every size, so bots use the routes too.
  5. Graphics: decide on raising the 1440×900 render cap on Retina screens.
  6. Rerun the balance gate, then a playtest.

The owner decides

  • The look-test corner, before the whole map is dressed.
  • The grey layout, before it is modelled.
Exit: the owner plays the new map, and it replaces the grey box.

4 · The generative forge

A player describes a hero and gets a body, an outfit, a weapon and projectiles that fit our skeleton and kit, and the Forge is done.

Do

  1. Meshes: a text or image prompt goes to the mesh service. The result is cleaned, scaled to the hero's size class, fitted to our skeleton and physique dials, skinned, and checked against the audit clips from session 1.
  2. Weapons: generated meshes snap to a hand socket with a grip offset. A compatibility rule matches each weapon class to its melee style or ranged kind, so a generated spear plays spear clips.
  3. Projectiles: a look per ranged kind (bolt, beam, cone, thrown), plus generated meshes for thrown objects, with muzzle and hand sockets.
  4. Safety and cost: a cap per player, content checks, caching, and a fallback to library parts when the service fails.
  5. Close the Forge in GAME.md §13.

The owner decides

  • Which service, Tripo or Meshy, plus API keys and approval to send prompts out. Nothing is sent before that.
  • The cost cap, and what players may generate.

Rule

  • The service is never named in player-facing text.
Exit: the owner forges a hero end to end and plays it on the new map.
