Kit library, and four sessions to close the game

Every button's library as it stands in the code today (307 items, generated from src/charter/templates), what it overlaps and what it misses for third-person hero combat, then the plan in the owner's order: lock the kit, build it and check it on today's map, rebuild the map, then the generative forge. 2026-11-04.

Is the library MECE? Not yet.

  • Not mutually exclusive. Transformations sit in both F and R under the same names; four E skills repeat the shared moves; some Q bursts repeat LMB weapons; the summon family holds no summons; stun lives outside the other controls.
  • Not collectively exhaustive for the genre. The hero-shooter core is well covered. Missing is most of what the anime and superhero fandoms come for: flight, swinging, teleporting to a mark, counters, clones, minions, copying a kit, grabs and throws, moveset-changing forms, revives and team-ups. Most need a new engine piece, not just new entries.

V

Melee weapon styles 7

Tap ×3 for the combo (the 3rd hit knocks back), hold for the charged heavy, X to guard. A style sets the clips, reach, arc and lunge; damage comes from the role. Every role may take every style.

Overlaps (not mutually exclusive)

  • Spear, whip and chain share Axe or staff with axes and staffs, though they move differently (thrust, crack and wrap against swing).

Missing for the genre (not exhaustive)

  • Dual blades and claws (Wolverine)
  • Spear or lance as its own thrusting style
  • Whip or chain as its own style
  • Scythe
  • Power gauntlets (could stay Unarmed with heavier blows)
  • An air juggle on the chain: launch, chase, slam (Sparking Zero, MHUR)
  • Grab, carry and throw (Lash's Death Slam, Sparking Zero throws)

LMB

Ranged 33

The weapon in the hero's hands: guns, bows, thrown weapons, wands, staffs and bare-hand magic. Ki Blast and Energy Wave live here. Weapons are read from each entry's name and tags; the code has no weapon field yet.

Overlaps (not mutually exclusive)

  • Q burst repeats some weapons: Snipe and Piercing Shot beside Rifle Shot and Railgun; Kunai Volley and Shuriken Fan beside Throwing Knives.
  • Longbow plays the rifle clips although the library has a full bow set.
  • Shoulder Cannon plays the rifle clips; a cannon needs a heavy carry.
  • Drone Zap plays a two-hand cast, but the drone is what fires.

Missing for the genre (not exhaustive)

  • Heavy guns: launcher, minigun, flamethrower (Punisher's turret, a spin-up gun)
  • Lobbed and bouncing grenades (Squirrel Girl's acorns); every projectile flies straight
  • Returning throws (Captain America's shield, Thor's hammer)
  • Hold to charge a shot (Grey Talon, Hawkeye, a charged Kamehameha)
  • Akimbo pistols
  • Arm cannon or repulsor in the palm (Iron Man)
  • Guided or map-wide shots (Grey Talon's Guided Owl)

Weapon families: one grip, one clip set

A family is a grip and a firing motion. Every weapon in it plays the same clips, so a new weapon only needs a family to be animated. The code today knows four styles (pistol, rifle, thrown, magic); the seven below are the proposal for session 1.

Spells 107

Everything no other row covers, in ten families: Burst 13 · Control 23 · Displacement 13 · Curse 14 · Zone 7 · Construct 4 · Summon 12 · Support 11 · Vision 6 · Trick 4.

Overlaps (not mutually exclusive)

  • The summon family holds no summons: it is turrets, traps and totems, which overlap construct and zone.
  • Stun is its own effect while the other eight controls share one type.
  • Venom Dart (curse) only deals damage, which makes it a burst.

Missing for the genre (not exhaustive)

  • Clones, decoys and illusions (Shadow Clone, Loki, Twice)
  • Minions that move and fight (Tokoyami's Dark Shadow, Naruto's summons)
  • Copying an enemy's form and kit (Loki's ultimate, Toga)
  • Possession: the target mirrors the caster (Shadow Possession)
  • A directional barrier that blocks shots (Doctor Strange, Magneto)
  • Absorbing projectiles (Magneto's ultimate)
  • Walkable terrain: ramps, ice paths, platforms (Todoroki)
  • Ability charges: two or three uses banked

Dash, blink, leap or grapple, one shared clip each with its own effects. Only movement skills move their caster.

Overlaps (not mutually exclusive)

  • Swapping places (Paradox)
  • Burrowing (Mo & Krill)
  • Carrying or riding an ally (Rocket on Groot, Jeff)
  • A homing dash to a target (Dragon Dash)

Ultimates 58

An overpowered move on a long cooldown, any kind of effect scaled up, with one custom clip and big effects.

Overlaps (not mutually exclusive)

  • Titan Form, Super Form, Ascended Form, Avatar Form and Shrink Form are transformations, which §4 puts in F.

Missing for the genre (not exhaustive)

  • Reviving downed allies (Adam Warlock; MHUR and Shinobi Striker revives)
  • Team-up moves between two named heroes (Marvel Rivals); synergies are role-count bonuses
  • Fusion (Sparking Zero)
  • Beam clashes (Sparking Zero)

MECE

Everything each button can hold

A proposal for session 1: one sorting rule between the buttons, then a grid inside each button, so any ability from any game or anime lands in exactly one cell. Built from what the genre's fans expect; "have" counts what our catalogue holds today.

Animation sources: what we can and can't use

  • Ripped game files are out. Animation files taken from Marvel Rivals or My Hero Ultra Rumble belong to their studios and publishers. That includes a friend's Drive folder and Sketchfab uploads under those names: the uploaders don't own them, so a download button doesn't grant a licence. Shipping them would also risk the whole game. I haven't opened or downloaded them.
  • What we can take from those games is reference: watching gameplay to judge timing, weight and silhouettes, as we did for the MHUR camera study. Our own clips can then be made or chosen to hit the same beats.
  • Where clips can come from: the library we have (Mixamo, Quaternius, CMU, 100STYLE: 1,283 clips); free packs whose licence allows games (to be checked one by one in session 1); recording our own moves with a phone and video-to-motion; and hand-keying in Blender the few iconic moves nothing else covers.

Outside the buttons: systems

Team-ups between two named heroes, fusion, beam clashes and a spendable power meter (ki, chakra, Plus Ultra) aren't abilities but rules about them. Each is a yes or no for the owner in session 1.

Plan

Four sessions to close the game

The owner's order, 2026-11-04. Each session ends with something the owner signs or plays. Movement feel is not matched to MHUR; we may differ. Every session runs the same gates (tsc, the fast suites, the balance gate alone, the build, the Vercel-shaped build, and a look with our own eyes) and records its decisions in GAME.md.

1 · Clip audit, and a locked kit library

Know that every clip we play reads right on every body, and turn the six button libraries into one list with no overlaps and no genre gaps the owner cares about.

Do

  1. An audit harness that scores each clip without a person watching: root drift, hip sway, foot slide, bone pops, loop seams and the travel line. It renders a contact sheet per clip on tiny, medium and huge bodies, female and male.
  2. Audit every clip in use: the shared moves (the table above), seven melee styles (three hits, heavy, guard), ranged in each stance, the four E kinds, the F toggle, R, hit reacts, knock-downs. Each gets keep, trim, replace or missing.
  3. Fix the five overlaps: forms in one row, E without baseline repeats, Q burst set apart from LMB, summon given real summons, stun folded into control.
  4. Write the library as a grid per button (delivery × effect group) so every item has exactly one cell, and list each gap as a new engine piece with a size and a yes or no.
  5. Attitude sets: each hero can carry a walk, run and idle with character (a strut, a swagger, a hip sway, a heavy brute's roll) and Forge and victory poses. The library has one strutting walk and about 25 hand-on-hip poses; the rest come from the free libraries or our own recordings.
  6. Lock the weapon families (the seven under LMB): every weapon belongs to one grip and one clip set; the bow gets its own set from the 16 bow clips we already have, and the heavy gun gets clips.
  7. Map it to bodies and archetypes: the clip each item plays per size class, the default kits per role, and which melee styles and ranged kinds each body may hold.

The owner decides

  • Which new pieces go in: teleport, counter, flight, clones and summons, moveset forms, grab and throw, air juggle, revive, charges, charge casts.
  • The form rule: F only, or F toggles and R is the bigger, timed form.
  • Whether E drops Wall Run, Combat Roll, Power Slide and Zipline.
  • New melee styles: spear, whip or chain, dual blades or claws, scythe.

Out of scope

  • Feel-matching to MHUR, map work, generated meshes.
Exit: the owner signs the locked library in GAME.md §4, with an audit report in which every clip in use passes or has its replacement named.

2 · Build the kit, then the owner's check on today's map

Everything signed in session 1 works in play, on every body, for players and bots, on the current map.

Do

  1. Build the approved pieces in build order (teleport and counters first), each with engine tests and a test that fails without it.
  2. Re-cut the catalogue to the locked grid: rename, move and remove entries; new entries for the new pieces.
  3. Swap in the replacement clips from the audit, and check every new move in the seat on tiny, medium and huge bodies with filmstrips.
  4. Teach the bots to use each new piece.
  5. Rerun the balance gate; retune only with the owner's say-so.

The owner decides

  • Combat and traversal on the current map: the owner's own check, and the fix list that comes from it.
  • Any retune the balance gate asks for.

If it runs over

  • Pieces later in the build order move to a short opening of session 3; the map does not start on an unchecked kit.
Exit: the owner has played combat and traversal on today's map and the fix list is closed.

3 · Rebuild the map around the kit

A map built for the moves we now have, in the arcade look, that every body size can travel.

Do

  1. A look-test corner first: the arcade direction (casino banners and indoor plants are fine, no Roman arena), lit and checked in renders.
  2. A grey-box layout made for the kit: ledges above 4.5 m where a grab should beat a mantle, marked walls for wall-runs, zipline and rail lines, room to fly if flight went in, sight lines for marksmen, low cover for crouch and prone.
  3. A traversal pass on every size class: each route checked in the seat.
  4. Model and bake in Blender, then navigation for every size, so bots use the routes too.
  5. Graphics: decide on raising the 1440×900 render cap on Retina screens.
  6. Rerun the balance gate, then a playtest.

The owner decides

  • The look-test corner, before the whole map is dressed.
  • The grey layout, before it is modelled.
Exit: the owner plays the new map, and it replaces the grey box.

4 · The generative forge

A player describes any human-like hero (a giant, a caped brawler, a muscle-bound mascot with a block for a head) and gets a mesh on our skeleton that plays every clip, with its weapon and projectiles.

Do

  1. The rule: joints in our places, anything on the surface. Two arms, two legs, a head on a neck; block heads, wood grain, suits, armour and cartoon proportions are fine. Tails, wings, four legs and extra arms are not.
  2. Generate: the prompt and any reference image become a spec (size class, proportions, surface, head, weapon, aura, a suggested kit), then a whole mesh in a T-pose, outfit included.
  3. Fit: find the mesh's joints, pick its size class and set our bone lengths to match, so short legs and huge hands keep their place.
  4. Skin: copy the weights from our standard body by nearest point; a hard part (a block head, armour plates) binds stiffly to its own bone.
  5. Head: attached at the neck socket (§9.1), the seam closed by the collar blend we already have. A face wrapped onto our head layout keeps the 52 face shapes; one that isn't keeps a painted face and still turns with the head.
  6. Gate: the mesh plays the session 1 audit clips on the physique bench (skin passing through itself, joint limits, foot slide) and is fixed or refused before the player sees it.
  7. Sex appeal for adult characters (owner, 2026-11-05): proportions, face, hair and a fitted outfit from the mesh; skin and hair shading that hold up close; bust, glute and thigh jiggle tuned per hero; the attitude sets from session 1. Never nude; a hero that reads as a child gets none of it, as the child preset already does.
  8. Weapons: generated, attached to the hand socket, and placed in a weapon family so it plays that family's clips.
  9. Projectiles: a look per ranged family, plus generated meshes for thrown objects.
  10. Cost and reliability: a cap per player, caching, and a fallback to a library body when the service fails; the one content check is the hard line above.
  11. Close the Forge in GAME.md §13.

The owner decides

  • Which service, Tripo or Meshy, plus API keys and approval to send prompts out. Nothing is sent before that.
  • Decided (owner, 2026-11-04): players generate exactly what they ask for, real people and famous characters included, with no restrictions; legal questions go to the owner's lawyers. One hard line stays in the generator: no nude or sexual depictions of real, identifiable people, and nothing sexual involving minors.
  • How the line is held (owner, 2026-11-04): explain, don't just refuse. The Forge's chat says what crossed the line and why, and offers the nearest thing it can make. Creative intent gets help: a nude statue parody of a celebrity becomes a draped marble-statue parody, a provocative satire keeps its point by other means. Clear bad intent gets a firm no, with no alternatives: a private person's photo with a sexual ask, repeated attempts after the explanation, or anything sexual with a minor. Repeat attempts slow the account down and flag it for review.
  • The cost cap.

Given up for free meshes

  • The physique dials, jiggle helpers and swappable clothes: the prompt does that job, and the outfit is part of the mesh.

Rule

  • The service is never named in player-facing text.
Exit: the owner forges a few very different heroes end to end (a giant, a slight caster, a mascot with a non-human head) and plays each on the new map.
