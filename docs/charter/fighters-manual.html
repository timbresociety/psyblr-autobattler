The Fighter’s Manual

Every hero in Psyblr runs on the same body of shared moves and seven buttons. Each button holds exactly one thing, and nothing sits on two buttons. This manual walks through all of it: how a fighter moves, climbs and rides the map, and how each button fights.

The numbers are the engine’s own values for a medium hero, at 60 ticks a second, in metres and seconds. Bigger and smaller heroes scale as the Sizes section shows. Anything marked stand-in works but still borrows another family’s animation. For the sorted list of every family on every button, see the Kit Library.

Each “See it” clip is the move as the game’s character view plays it, recorded on our own medium body with a one-hand edge and a one-hand gun, one frame every 1/30 s. Under each clip are the animation clips it plays. Click a clip to pause it.

Basics

One rule sorts every ability

Two questions decide which button an ability lives on: what it costs to use, and what it’s for. Each pair of answers belongs to exactly one button, so there are no overlaps and no gaps.

ButtonPaid for byUsed forNever holds
SharedNothing; every hero has itMoving, defending, recoveringAnything only some heroes have
VNothing; the held weaponHitting up closeShots, spells
LMBA magazine; the held weaponHitting at range, again and againCooldown nukes
EA cooldownMoving the caster, including shared moves done betterAnything that doesn’t move the caster
QA cooldownActing on others or on the worldSelf-only buffs, movement
FIts own meter, drained while onAn aura on the caster and on allies near itForms, one-shot actions
RA long cooldownThe one big moment: an overpowered skill, or a form with new movesAnything a short cooldown could pay for

The engine enforces this. A melee slot holds only a weapon style, ranged holds only repeat fire from the hand or a weapon, Q holds nothing melee or self-only, E holds only things that move the caster, and F never holds a form.

SharedTraversal

In the air

Jumps are big and anime-sized, and the player steers them. Keys steer the body in the air, and letting go brakes the drift, so you decide how far a jump goes. A jump always rises its full height, however briefly Space is pressed. A sprint’s jump keeps its take-off speed, so it flies further than a jog’s.

The double jump adds height and range and nothing else: no flip, and no roll on landing. Every attack works in the air, and ordinary map heights never deal fall damage.

How the map is built around these moves. Baseline movement reaches every major platform. Every strong position has two or three routes in (stairs, a jump chain and a side flank). VCombat

V · Melee

V holds a weapon family, chosen by how the weapon lands its hits rather than by its name, so a scythe swings like an axe. The family sets the animations, reach, contact frames and lunge. Damage comes from the role’s weapon damage. Every family fills the same moveset, so heroes with different weapons still play alike.

Blows home in on the target in front (up to 70° off your aim, lunging over 0.12 s), and stop briefly on contact so the hit lands with weight.

FCombat

F · Aura

F switches an aura on and off. While on, it drains its own meter, shown on the HUD, and shows round the body. It refills while off and switches itself off when dry. It never costs health and never blocks an action. An aura never changes the body or the moveset; that belongs to R.

Systems

These are rules about abilities, not abilities in their own right.

SystemHow it works
Team-upStand by an ally (within 2.2 m) and press interact: they throw you 12 m along your aim over 0.7 s, in a 3 m arc. You land for 80 damage on everyone within 2.5 m. Once every 10 s.
FusionAn R that merges you with the nearest ally within 4 m for a while: hits land 50% harder, with a shield worth half the ally’s health.
Beam clashTwo held beams that meet push against each other. The clash point moves toward the weaker side at 35% of the way a second, and every fresh press adds 12%. When it reaches a caster, that beam breaks and its caster takes 3× the winner’s beam damage.
Power meterA spendable meter that fills as you deal damage (a full bar for your own health’s worth) and take it (one and a half times that). Some Q casts spend it.
Ability chargesTwo or three uses banked: two go off back to back, the third waits.
Hold to chargeOn LMB and on Q: hold to draw the cast back, release to let go. A Counter can cancel a charge in progress.
The body

Weapons, and where they’re carried

A hero holds at most one melee weapon (V) and one ranged weapon (LMB). The button in use has its weapon in hand, and the other waits in its carry slot: a blow brings the melee weapon out, and a shot brings the ranged one. Hangs, climbs and ziplines stow both. Fists, kicks, worn weapons such as gauntlets or claws, bare palms and body emitters need no slot.

Each carry kind ranks its slots. The hero’s two weapons get the assignment with the lowest total rank in which no slot is used twice, and a pair always moves together. All 100 pairings of the ten melee carry kinds against the ten ranged ones place without a clash. A bow with dual swords puts the bow on the back and moves the swords to the hips.

Attitude sets change how a hero moves and fights, not how it stands still. Each set covers the walk, jog and sprint, the strafe, take-off and landing weight, and the guard-up walk between strikes. It’s a layer over every animation: a tipped spine and head, arms held out, bent knees, a bigger hip and chest swing on the walk, and the set’s own stride beat. Moves at the floor (landings, rolls, the slide, the dive, prone) and hanging and climbing play without the layer, so hands and feet still meet the ground and the ledge.

Where it stands

Every mechanic above runs in John’s lab, the test room where one character carries every weapon, button pick, size and attitude. The newer pieces (the dash, air, slam, launcher and grab blows, the perfect guard, knockdowns and revives, wall-runs, the new ranged families and flights, and the Systems) are switched on there. The automated balance runs don’t use them yet.

Where the animation work stands, and what’s left for looks in the next two sessions:

  • Every action has its own animation. Each of the eleven melee families plays its own chain, heavy, dash, air, slam and launcher blows and its own R flurry (the grab is one throw for all). Each gun and spell grip fires its own way for single shots, bursts, full auto, spreads, beams and charges. Each Q shape casts its own way, each E kind moves its own way, each aura kind switches on with its own flourish, and each R shape starts its own way. The galleries above show every one; the Kit Library lists them.
  • Fixed along the way: Chop and reap no longer opens on a kick (the axe set’s kicks are gone from it). The lab’s allies aura now really reaches the allies near you, so it switches on as an allies aura. A skill’s or blow’s clip no longer carries the body off its spot while the engine moves it too: the hips are held near where the clip starts.
  • Fixed on 12 November: summons now appear in John’s lab (minions, a clone, a decoy, the titan): the lab gave John his kit after the match had already set aside its summons’ bodies. The one-hand gun fires pistol_fire. Paired guns fire with a gun in each hand: the off arm mirrors the main one half a beat behind, each arm turned out to its own side and each barrel straight ahead. A long motion-captured clip (a combo, a long cast) plays only round its blow instead of being squeezed whole into one cast. Possession reaches out and holds the target (pointing_forward), a turret is knelt down and built (ual_fixing_kneeling), the mirror guard holds its ward up (magic_standing_block_idle), swapping places is a quick step aside (standing_dodge_left) instead of an evade that ended flat on the ground, and a leap lands on its feet (cmu_standing_long_jump) instead of diving into a roll.
  • The whip is rigid; it has no rope physics yet.
  • Team-ups are one generic throw for now, and flight doesn’t have a camera of its own yet.
  • Fixed while recording these clips: until a hero first ran, the walk was blended half-and-half into every other move (a hang held its arms out straight; a jump kept a walking stride), and a finished held cast stayed blended into the next held cast. Both are fixed in the character view.
  • Carry looks: a spear stows head-down, a bow lies flat on the back, and held blades cross the body in the idle stance.
