The Kit Library

Every ability a Psyblr hero can have, sorted so that nothing sits in two places and nothing is left out. Two questions put each ability on exactly one button. Inside each button, a fixed order puts it in exactly one family.

This is the library as signed at the end of session 1 (GAME.md §4, 10 November 2026), with the six calls of 7 November and the five settled at the signature. Each family also lists its options in John’s lab, where every one of them can be tried, and the animation it plays. For how each move plays and its numbers, see the Fighter’s Manual.

How it sorts

What was decided

The owner’s six calls, 7 November

  1. New mechanics: yes to all of them, as long as each fits a button, with no overlaps and no gaps.
  2. F is an aura, R is the ultimate. F never holds a form.
  3. E keeps enhanced shared moves, as long as the E version does more and carries its own name.
  4. Melee families come from the combat action, not the weapon’s name: a scythe plays the axe’s swings.
  5. Weapons have default carry slots that never clash.
  6. Attitude sets cover combat and traversal only for now; idles and poses wait.

Settled with the signature, 10 November

  1. Jump pads: in, as shared map pieces.
  2. Kit switch: R’s eighth shape, apart from forms.
  3. Alternate fire: left out for now; right mouse stays first-person aim.
  4. Staff, thrust and flexible clips: recorded on a phone and turned into clips.
  5. Bodies that sink into the floor: fixed in session 2 (13 November): hips by leg length, toes kept up, lying moves lifted by the body’s depth.

EMovement skill

Movement skills, by how they move the caster

An E skill is either a way of moving the shared moves lack, or a shared move done better. The better version must do something the shared one can’t, and carries its own name. What happens on arrival (nothing, damage, control or a shield) is the skill’s effect, not its kind. Each kind has one clip, the same for every hero that moves that way.

FAura

Auras

An F skill is an aura you switch on and off. While on, it drains its own meter and shows round the body; when the meter runs dry it switches itself off. It never changes the body or the moveset; those are R’s. Each aura has one trigger: while on, on hit, every third hit, at low health, on a kill, or when hit.

Enforced

Typing rules

The engine enforces these, so a wrong item can’t be stored on a button:

  • V holds only a weapon family.
  • LMB holds only things that fire from the hand or a weapon, on repeat.
  • Q holds nothing melee and nothing self-only.
  • F holds nothing that reaches anyone else, except an aura given to allies near the caster, and never a form.
  • E holds only things that move the caster.
Check

Why it’s MECE

Mutually exclusive. The two questions (paid by, and for) have one answer each, and each pair belongs to one button. Inside a button, every family list is an ordered test: an item takes the first family it fits, so even an item that could fit two lands in exactly one. A scythe is Chop and reap, never Two-hand edge. A stun with damage is Control, never Burst. A swap of places is E, never Displacement.

Collectively exhaustive. Every list ends in a catch-all: V by how the weapon hits, LMB by the grip, Q with Burst (pure damage), and R with eight shapes that between them cover one target, an area now, an area over time, the team, the caster, a new body, a new ally and a new kit. What no button may hold (a self-buff on Q, a form on F) is named in the rule table, so it has a home elsewhere.

The engine sorts the same way: lab options are checked in tests to land in their own family, kind or shape, and no two families on a button start with the same animation.
